## Visual research and boundaries

- **R1 — Hugo van Gelderen / Anefo, Nationaal Archief, [Willem II and United, 25 September 1963](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WillemIIManchesterUnited1963b.jpg)**. CC0 1.0; period face/kit reference. The photograph is in Rotterdam: it is explicitly **not** used as Old Trafford architecture. Local private reference: `private/united-1963.jpg`.
- **R2 — Chelsea FC, [1970 final replay briefing](https://www.chelseafc.com/en/news/article/50th-anniversary-pre-match-briefing--1970-fa-cup-final-replay)**, accompanying archive image `old_trafford_1966.jpg`. Aerial geometry studied for the old covered Stretford End, open corners, perimeter and floodlight towers; image not redistributed. Rights remain with its source. The new cantilever side is not assigned wholesale to 1964.
- **R3 — [Manchester Devils translation of ManUtd.com stadium history](https://www.manchesterdevils.com/articles/lire/3292/old-trafford-1963-1974-3)**: redevelopment during 1964–66; [preceding section](https://www.manchesterdevils.com/articles/lire/3291/old-trafford-1931-1962-2) records the Stretford End cover in 1959. Date descriptions differ between removing the old roof and opening the replacement; drawings therefore show the older end terrace, not an exact dated panorama of all four sides.
- **R4 — Manchester United, [Inside United: Remembering The King](https://www.manutd.com/en/news/inside-united-march-2025-edition-pays-tribute-to-denis-law)**, describes the red-and-white kit in a 1962/63 portrait. Club scenes use plain red crewneck/white trim, white shorts, black socks/white turnover and black leather boots, consistent with R1. No sponsors or modern manufacturers' graphics. Opponents' white/navy kit is a contrasting generic period kit, not an asserted named fixture.
- **R5 — Scottish FA profile photo** is a later collared Scotland shirt, viewed for face only; it must **not** supply a 1964 collar. Early-1960s navy shirt/white crewneck and white shorts checked against [the match-worn shirt listing](https://www.easyliveauction.com/catalogue/lot/39d27549807482e955def25d9fd79df8/1f1638c9faeb7c2641b7b1e9c67628d0/two-day-sale-of-football-memorabilia/) and [1961 archive caption](https://prints.colorsport.co.uk/football/denis-law-scotland-1961-5745325.html). National portrait uses anonymous blurred terraces: no invented identifiable Scottish stadium or Edinburgh skyline. The auction says early1960s, not an exact1964 match.
- Greater Manchester Police's Flickr aerial was rejected as a period reference: its own caption estimates early1980s. Draft art's rows of tall residential buildings/chimneys and Scottish church skyline were rejected after the owner's historical-detail clarification.

The final art retains a family3D interpretation. Period wool/cotton, worn turf, flat gray light, standing crowds and low pale barriers replace fantasy architecture. Host clothing is the established fictional cast and intentionally unchanged. The golden-football emblem is Storygro's original SVG, not an official award logo. No generated lettering, numerals or crests; all titles/statistics are sourced overlays. ## Voice and pronunciation

OmniVoice B, speed0.90, stress off, original synthetic reference. [Лоу, Денис](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Лоу,_Денис) gives Де́нис Ло́у: the Scottish player differs from Russian Дени́с. Shared dictionary records the phonetic alias Дэнис; the script hint preserves the displayed spelling Денис. Ча́рльтон, Бо́бби and name forms are recorded in the shared lexicon. Three contextual probes in voice-bench.yaml are diagnostic, not owner listening approval or proof of correct stress. Technical word matching cannot establish stress.
