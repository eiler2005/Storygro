# Sources — «Кто разбогатеет на ИИ» / "Who gets rich on AI"

The episode retells a post by the owner (26 October 2026) about Greenspan and Wooldridge's
*Capitalism in America* and the AI build-out. Every number that appears on screen is listed here with
its quote; the argument the post makes is labelled as an argument, not as a measurement.

## On screen

### 1873: the railway panic

- **89 of 364 US railroads went bankrupt.**
  [PBS, *American Experience*, "The Panic of 1873"](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/general-article/grant-panic):
  "89 of the country's 364 railroads crashed into bankruptcy". The same page dates the failure of
  **Jay Cooke & Company to 18 September 1873** and reports about **18,000 business failures** and
  unemployment of **14% by 1876**.
  *Caveat:* this is a secondary source; it gives no scholarly citation for the count. The figure is
  widely repeated but we did not trace it to the primary railway returns. The New York Stock Exchange
  closing for ten days after Cooke's failure is standard in the literature on the panic and is said in
  the narration, not shown as a number on screen.

### The book and its author

- **Alan Greenspan, Adrian Wooldridge, *Capitalism in America: A History*, Penguin Press, 16 October
  2018** (ISBN 978-0-7352-2244-1); Russian edition «Капитализм в Америке. История», Альпина Паблишер —
  [publisher's page](https://alpinabook.ru/catalog/book-kapitalizm-v-amerike/) (the link in the owner's
  post). Wooldridge was political editor of *The Economist* and wrote its Bagehot column.
- **Greenspan chaired the Federal Reserve from 11 August 1987 to 31 January 2006** — 18 years and
  5 months, which the narration calls "almost nineteen years" and the badge shows as the span
  `1987–2006`.
- **"I found a flaw."** Testimony to the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform,
  **23 October 2008**, answering Rep. Henry Waxman
  ([NPR transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/96070766)): "I found a flaw. I don't know how
  significant or permanent it is. But I have been very distressed by that fact," and, on the flaw
  being in "the model that I perceived is a critical functioning structure that defines how the world
  works". Asked whether his view of the world had been wrong, he answered "Precisely."
  *Caveat:* transcripts differ slightly ("a critical" / "the critical"); we show only the four-word
  phrase, which is identical in every transcript. We did not check the committee's own published
  record.

### The AI build-out

- **About $725 billion of planned 2026 capital spending by Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Meta.**
  [Yahoo Finance, Myles Udland, 30 April 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/magnificent-7-earnings-rush-reveals-ai-spending-surge-with-hyperscaler-capex-set-to-reach-725-billion-in-2026-224901707.html):
  "As of Wednesday night's reports, that number is closer to $725 billion", against a pre-earnings
  high-end estimate of about $670 billion for the big four hyperscalers.
  *Caveats, and they matter:*
  - **This is guidance, not money already spent.** The badge and the narration both say "planned".
  - **The owner's post attributes the figure to Bloomberg. We could not confirm that attribution** and
    did not find a Bloomberg item with this number; the sourced one we did find is the Yahoo Finance
    report above, so the episode cites that. A secondary aggregator also credits a *Financial Times*
    compilation of the same Q1 earnings.
  - **The number moved during 2026.** Bridgewater's February estimate reported by Reuters was about
    $650 billion; CNBC in February put it near $700 billion; later 2026 tallies sum company guidance to
    roughly $730 billion and ranges of $720–745 billion circulate. We show the April figure with its
    date and call it a plan.

- **50.9% of US household corporate equities and mutual fund shares held by the top 1%.**
  Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, *Distributional Financial Accounts*, series
  [WFRBST01122](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WFRBST01122): 50.9 percent of aggregate, **Q2 2026**,
  not seasonally adjusted; the FRED page was updated 18 September 2026 (50.6 in Q1 2026). The owner's
  post says "около 51%", which is this figure rounded.
  *Caveat:* the Fed notes that from 2026Q2 the Financial Accounts show hedge fund and private debt fund
  shares separately, but the DFA has not adopted that change, so those holdings are still counted inside
  "corporate equities and mutual funds".

## Argument, not measurement

These are on screen as words, and `facts.json` marks them as our own wording of the owner's post:

- **the specialist who needed a week and now needs a day** — an illustrative example. No study is
  cited and none is implied; the label on the frame says so.
- **who keeps the gain** (client / employer / model vendor / the worker as an open question) — a chain
  of reasoning from the post, not a measured distribution.
- **"hours → an asset"** — the post's central argument about a possibility, not a forecast. The episode
  keeps the post's own counter-scenario (concentration at a few corporations) and its caveat that AI
  lowers the cost of building a product but does not create demand for it.

## Deliberately left out

- The post's phrase about the scale of investment resembling the industrial revolution: the brand's
  style rules avoid "революция" as hype, and the comparison adds no checkable fact.
- The 2008 crisis as a judgement on Greenspan's record. The episode quotes only what he said himself.
- Any claim about how much a subscription to a model costs beyond "tens of dollars a month", which is
  the post's own wording and is not shown as a number on screen.
- Anything about who will in fact capture the gains. The episode says the question is open, because
  that is what the sources support.
