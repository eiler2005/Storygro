# Produce a Football Legends episode

The owner established a shared first-frame identity and distinct historical worlds on 30 October 2026. The [series bible](../../content/series/football-legends.md) is the editorial source. These directions establish the format, not an owner approval of a particular script, storyboard or release.

**Owner amendment,31 October2026:** the actual player appears with Matvey and Sofia in the opening artwork, as explicitly required in the supplied episode9–15 documents. New/remade episodes also include real player and relevant venue photographs as dated inserts alongside the illustrations. Prefer verified public-domain/CC0 or compatible licensed files; inspect the file-specific licence, author and actual photo date. A search result or an old photograph alone does not establish reuse rights. Keep original files off Git and record source/licence URLs, author, photo/upload dates, SHA-256, where the photo appears and any display crop in `photos.json`, `sources.md`, `CREDITS.md` and the delivered post. Use `archive-photo` with text bound to facts. Preserve the photographic content; no generative retouch, recolouring or fabricated match detail. A later view of a stadium must carry its real date and cannot stand in for an archival match frame. If a suitable period file cannot be found, use an eligible dated alternative and explain that limit. Episode9 uses an Anefo portrait from1963 (CC0) and Simon Paul's stadium photo from summer2003 (author's public-domain release). These photo inserts are an additional FL-R1 confounder, recorded in its direction plan.

The owner adopted the [31 October research](../../content/research/reels-growth-2026-10-31/README.md) as a required [retention direction worksheet and learning loop](reels-retention.md). Complete `DIRECTION.md` before producing assets. In new episodes, stage a clear problem in the first background while retaining the entire identity; give an early result, develop a causal action and resolve the promise. The next newly commissioned episode uses **FL-R1: action beneath the opening identity**, with the duration band, narrator and ending approach held fixed. Test one main change at a time. A 45–60-second adaptation is a later separate hypothesis, not a replacement for a supplied verbatim script or the current length. Compare the published revision, actual post age and distribution mode; the early Sivori view count does not prove a cause.

**Audience-facing correction by the owner,31 October2026:** keep the children's cut focused on the story. On photos show only a name/place and a short date when needed; omit photographer usernames, CC0/Public-domain codes and other production metadata. Full attribution and rights evidence stay in the description and repository records, under the selected file's actual terms. This supersedes the earlier suggestion to put author/licence lines in photo cards. Review guides never appear in exported video. The reusable `archive-photo` credit prop is optional; omit it for this series.

**Episode9 pacing revision:** after viewing v1 the owner requested slower speech and more time to inspect the picture, especially after the 5:3 result. V2 keeps OmniVoice B and uses 0.82/off, with a 1.60-second post-speech final-score hold. This is an explicit episode override; future episodes retain the standing voice profile unless changed by the owner. Record the changed speed, holds and duration as confounders rather than describing FL-R1 as a matched test.

**Owner adoption after V2, 31 October 2026:** apply [calm children's pacing](reels-retention.md#pace-for-children) to future/newly remade episodes: allow time to inspect the scene and understand the result, hold the payoff after the spoken phrase, and check photo dwell time on the real timeline. Use Eusebio V2 as a working pacing reference; approximately 1–2 seconds after a decisive result is a review starting point, not a cut schedule. Keep the selected narrator and explicit episode settings, check difficult words in context and leave final listening pending when unavailable. This confirms the production method without recording a release approval or a retention result.

1. Start with `storygro new content/videos/<id> --style cartoon --lang ru --target instagram-reels`. Keep the selected narrator and original host references.
2. Research the player's period face, club and national kits, venue and story. Write a short visual brief: place, era, light, palette, distinct teammates/opponents and at least three story actions. Different episodes must have different settings. Generate through the built-in GPT Image tool; record the exact prompts, references and checksums in `images.json`. Record the model identifier as undisclosed when the tool does not expose it. Generated images contain no lettering, numbers or logos.
3. Import `/rc/styles/cartoon/football-legends.css` in the episode theme. Override palette tokens for the episode; retain the common fonts, caption treatment and identity hierarchy. The golden-football symbol is an original SVG, not an official organiser logo.
4. Open with `scene: series-cover`. Bind `projectFrom: facts.series.project`, `titleFrom: facts.series.title` and **`subtitleFrom: facts.series.episode` containing the player's name** (owner amendment, 31 October 2026). Frame zero must show «Маленькие большие дела», «Легенды футбола», the player name and golden-football symbol together. Use `image` for episode-specific host art and `emblem: /rc/styles/cartoon/golden-ball.svg`. Optional `reveal` and `revealAt` switch to the story shot on a narration cue. Compose heads and ears below the enlarged title card, with all faces/title inside the central square. The complete identity is readable at frame zero; no animated entrance or silent logo pre-roll.
5. For an authorised retention experiment, state the testable question in the opening, start narration immediately, change view on narrative actions, and answer the opening question before the final viewer question. Keep the source's nuance. Do not invent a surprising causal claim or promise improved reach. Episode 6 adds a hook while retaining the supplied body and final question.
6. Run the stress audit, review its dictionary diff, compare difficult names, and listen. ASR spelling and vowel recognition are not a stress verdict. Inspect all shots/cues and captions, first/last MP4 frames, and feed/cover previews. Check reverse seeking. The full-frame background stays native 1080×1920; titles and faces fit the central square at the opening.
7. Record agent approvals only as technical. Regenerate the plan/tracker, render after the last input change, then deliver locally. Include the spec's post text and photo attribution in the delivered description (episode9 supplies `POST.txt` plus photo records; the flat `deliver` command itself copies media/upload notes). Verify extra delivered files in its manifest. Review questions remain in `review.yaml` until the owner answers. Follow the [Instagram upload instructions](instagram-without-cropping.md#upload) only after explicit publication authorisation.

Episode 5 applies the shared cover to Turin without changing its selected narration. [Episode 6](../../content/videos/football-legends-06-masopust/README.md) pilots the retention structure in Santiago/Prague. Older delivered episodes are revised deliberately, not silently overwritten. After a named publication, manual metrics can compare early retention, average watch time and completion at comparable durations; no uplift has yet been measured.

[Episode 7](../../content/videos/football-legends-07-yashin/README.md) uses staged goalkeeper actions for suspense: approaching shot, wide dive, glove/ball close-up, interception and defensive communication. Keep the ball fully visible, maintain goal/kit anatomy and move individual captions into clear background space in action close-ups. Its wet emerald/sapphire/gold Moscow atmosphere is an artistic choice, not weather evidence for a named game. The supplied body remains intact, with only an explicitly requested action hook added.

Keep a short answer with its question and a conjunction-led clause with its preceding sentence when isolated takes truncate or lose words. Change the illustration on an aligned cue inside that narration beat. Preserve every supplied word, verify the replacement, and rerun full-file speech QA; do not lower its threshold to accept a fragment.

[Episode 8](../../content/videos/football-legends-08-law/README.md) adds the mandatory player name using the existing subtitle contract. Its historical-detail pass uses period kit references and old Stretford End geometry: distinguish the older end terrace from the side stand rebuilt during the mid1960s. Record the date and role of every archive reference; a later photograph may support an unchanged detail, not the whole earlier panorama. Remove fantasy city skylines, modern sponsors and generic stadium architecture. Keep the family3D medium and established host clothing; historical closeness does not imply an exact match reconstruction. Earlier delivered episodes are not silently reissued.

The owner's further cover amendment (31 October 2026) adds a **verified nickname or distinctive trait plus a recognisable playful visual idea**, starting with Law's «Король»/The King and a slightly tilted crown. Put the nickname/trait with the player name in the data-bound subtitle; retain both shared titles and the football emblem. Research an actual nickname before presenting it as one; if none is established, use a clearly editorial skill/trait description. Develop a different metaphor for each episode, tied to its story. A comic accessory is a visual metaphor, not evidence of a historical costume or event: retain the period kit, venue and cast underneath it. Recheck the central square with the new accessory and extra title length. Apply to subsequent/newly remade episodes.

Keep the metaphor visibly separate from anatomy, preserve the hosts and voice, and recheck the first-frame square crop.
