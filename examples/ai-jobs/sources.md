### [S1] Stanford Digital Economy Lab — "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (August 2026)
Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen. *Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence.* Stanford Digital Economy Lab and NBER, PDF dated 12 Aug 2026, 140 pp. ADP payroll records, US, balanced panel of firms, Nov 2022 – Jun 2026.
<https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf>

- "We find no evidence of widespread, economy-wide job displacement."
- "In levels, employment of 22–25-year-olds in the two most exposed quintiles fell about 11% between November 2022 and June 2026, while employment of the same age group in the three least-exposed quintiles grew about 10%." → **−11% / +10%**
- "It operates primarily through reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations." → *"не увольняют — меньше нанимают"*
- "AI may be automating the checkable, process-intensive tasks that historically justified entry-level headcount, while increasing the leverage of experienced staff." → the task-stack and ladder scenes
- Caveat, quoted: results "attenuate when controlling for education, show some divergent trends predating generative AI, and are more pronounced in the ADP analysis sample than in national survey benchmarks … We interpret these facts as early, descriptive indicators — canaries in the coal mine — rather than causal estimates." → the on-screen source line says *"ранний сигнал, не доказанная причина"*.
- Do not mix versions: Aug 2025 headlined a 13 % regression estimate, Nov 2025 16 %; the 2026 version uses a "kept-pace" gap of 19 %.

**Verified** 2026-10-18 in the PDF text (the Stanford host reset connections from the production machine; the identical PDF was read from an archived copy, title, authors and date checked).

## Research notes (summary)

The research pass collected evidence for and against the thesis "AI automates tasks, not jobs; the clearest early harm is at the entry level":

- **Supported:** exposure is not loss (IMF, ILO, Anthropic's 94 % vs 33 %); no economy-wide displacement in payroll data; a hiring-side decline for 22–25-year-olds in exposed jobs (Stanford), junior employment ~9 % lower at firms adopting GenAI (Hosseini & Lichtinger, Harvard, SSRN 5425555 — reported, not read in full).
- **Not established:** causation (pre-trends, education confound, weaker in national surveys); OECD, Yale Budget Lab and Brookings do not attribute weak youth outcomes to AI; Danish register data show null effects on earnings and hours.
- **Complication:** AI raises novices' productivity most in experiments (customer support +34 % for novices in the NBER version, +15 % average in QJE). Firms can still need fewer novices per unit of output — the episode's mechanism.
- **Russia:** unemployment about 2.1–2.2 % (Rosstat via hh.ru, spring 2026); IT vacancies −35–37 % year on year with 22–23 resumes per IT vacancy (hh.ru monthly reports). No Russian study attributes the junior squeeze to AI; the Russian version does not claim one.
- **Predictions are labelled as predictions:** e.g. Dario Amodei (Axios, 28 May 2025) on entry-level white-collar jobs.
